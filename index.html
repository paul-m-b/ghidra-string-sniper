Introduction / Proposal

Reverse engineering proprietary binaries is often hindered by stripped symbols, making it difficult to quickly understand a program’s structure and behavior. Analysts can end up wasting significant time reverse engineering common libraries or relying on guesswork, which may lead to inaccurate conclusions. However, even stripped binaries frequently contain useful strings. These strings can provide valuable hints about underlying functionality, libraries, or protocols in use. Tools like Sourcegraph enable large scale searching across public repositories, making them especially useful for correlating these strings with real world implementations. By searching for distinctive strings extracted from a binary, it is often possible to identify matching codebases or libraries. From there, reverse engineers can import relevant headers, symbols, and structures—accelerating analysis and improving accuracy.

Proprosed Solution

  1. Extract and identify meaningful or unique strings from a binary
  2. Query Sourcegraph for relevant code matches
  3. Use an LLM to correlate search results with the binary’s assembly or decompiled output
  4. Present likely matches to the analyst for manual or automated application

Overall Goal

The goal is to reduce time spent on redundant analysis, improve identification of known components, and provide a clearer high level understanding of a target binary early in the reverse engineering process.
